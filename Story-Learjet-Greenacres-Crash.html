A Learjet broke apart over Palm Beach County and dived into Military Trail, killing all nine on board

On Sunday 1 November a charter jet bound for the Bahamas reported ice and turbulence, called mayday and fell about 26,500 feet in one minute. Investigators say part of its tail came off in the air. The cockpit voice recorder is in their lab. No cause has been named yet.

How it unfolded

Oldest first. Every entry carries its sources.

NEW 1 Nov, about 12:49 pm Eastern

A Learjet 60 business jet (registration N722BD, built 2004) dived from about 35,000 feet and hit Military Trail at 10th Avenue North in unincorporated Palm Beach County, near Greenacres, a few miles south of the main West Palm Beach airport. All nine people on board died: two crew and seven passengers. It was a charter flight from Austin, Texas, to North Eleuthera in the Bahamas, run by Aurora Aviation of Fort Lauderdale, which trades as Upper Jets. It missed homes in the Greenacres Mobile Home Community by feet and left a crater in the road. One person in a passing car was hurt. A dashcam filmed the impact.

Sources: PBS NewsHour (AP) Flightradar24 FlightGlobal
UPDATED 1 to 3 Nov, families and records

A family spokesperson named the passengers: Austin technology entrepreneur Adam Robinson, his wife Helen Sharp, their young children Emma and Charlie Robinson, family friends Rachael Reed and Charmi Fehr, and the children's nanny Anita Maria Cetina. The captain was Jeff Gregory; the first officer was Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, 20, of Boca Raton. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) records show she held a commercial pilot certificate and a rating to fly the Learjet 60 as second in command, not as captain. It is the deadliest Learjet 60 crash on record.

Sources: KUT Austin Flying Magazine CNN, 2 Nov
UPDATED 2 and 3 Nov, National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) briefings

NTSB board member Tom Chapman: "We do have some indication that the aircraft was breaking up before impact." On 3 Nov he confirmed part of the tail came off in the air: the left elevator (the tail surface that pitches the nose up and down) was found in three pieces, 1.3 to 3.8 miles from the crater. Air traffic audio shows the crew reported light clear ice and moderate turbulence at about 35,000 feet, with height swings of about 700 feet, and asked to climb to 37,000 feet. Then came an overspeed, a mayday call and a dive faster than 20,000 feet a minute. The FAA says the jet fell from 35,250 to 8,750 feet in one minute. Thunderstorms were in the area. The NTSB says weather is a main focus but has not said it caused the crash.

Sources: ABC News WFTV CBS12 Fox News
UPDATED 4 to 9 Nov, digging out the crater

Digging began late on 4 Nov in a crater that kept filling with storm water; part of one engine was found on 5 Nov. The NTSB asked anyone holding debris, including pieces found on roofs, not to keep or move it and to email witness@ntsb.gov. By 8 Nov it had recovered parts of both wings, both engines, the flight deck, the vertical and horizontal tail, the left elevator, aileron sections and flap tracks; some went to its Washington laboratories. On 9 Nov it said the cockpit voice recorder had been found at the impact point and the dig was finished. The recorder is being checked in the NTSB lab; whether it captured usable audio has not been said.

Sources: NTSB investigation page CNN, 5 Nov CNN, 9 Nov KTLA
UPDATED 1 to 12 Nov, on the ground

About 600 residents of the mobile home community were evacuated or displaced; shelters opened and United Way set up a relief fund. John I. Leonard High School, next to the site, closed on 2 Nov and reopened later that week. Residents began going home around 9 Nov. Military Trail, cut by the crater, reopened in both directions on 12 Nov after repairs, the Florida Department of Transportation said.

Sources: WPTV WPBF Wikipedia summary
NO CHANGE 21 Nov check

No new NTSB statement since 9 Nov. No cause has been named. The NTSB usually issues a preliminary report about 30 days after a crash, which would be around early December, but it has not announced a date. A final report with the probable cause usually takes one to two years. No lawsuit or FAA action on the Learjet 60 was found in this scan.

Sources: NTSB investigation page WPBF

What investigators have said

  • The jet showed signs of breaking up before impact (Tom Chapman, NTSB board member, 2 Nov).
  • Part of the tail separated in the air; the left elevator was found in three pieces 1.3 to 3.8 miles away (3 Nov).
  • Weather is a main focus. The crew reported ice and turbulence; weather analysts (not the NTSB) say the jet passed through a strong storm cell minutes before the dive.
  • Wreckage from both wings, both engines, the flight deck and the tail has been recovered; some is in the NTSB laboratories (8 Nov).
  • The cockpit voice recorder was found at the impact point and is being checked in the NTSB lab (9 Nov).
  • Anyone holding debris should not keep or move it and should email witness@ntsb.gov with a photo.

What has NOT been said yet

  • What caused the crash. The NTSB says it is too early.
  • Whether the tail broke first and caused the dive, or broke because of the dive.
  • Whether the voice recorder captured usable audio.
  • The date of the preliminary report. The NTSB usually issues one about 30 days after a crash.
  • Any lawsuit, or any FAA action on Learjet 60 jets: none found as of 21 Nov.
  • A family account (the first officer texted "I've got turbulence, I've got ice" about 10 minutes before) comes from her stepfather, not from investigators.
