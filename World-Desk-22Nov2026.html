HEADLINE · SCIENCE

Computer proof checkers back the Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

The Riemann Hypothesis is a 167-year-old question about where the prime numbers hide, and one of seven problems with a $1 million prize. On Friday 20 Nov, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge said he and the AI (artificial intelligence) model Claude had proved it. Now the Lean and Mathlib communities, who run the main software that checks proofs line by line, say in a joint statement they have "full confidence" it is correct. Human experts still need weeks to understand the ideas.

What the hypothesis says

The zeta function has infinitely many "zeros", points where it equals nothing. Riemann guessed in 1859 that every interesting zero sits exactly on the yellow line. Press play to plot the first ten, at their real heights.
